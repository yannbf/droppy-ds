# Droppy brand guidelines

Droppy is the design system and brand identity of Mealdrop Corp, and it powers our digital
products and marketing materials. Our two products both use Droppy extensively:


<dl>
  <dt>MealDrop</dt>
  <dd>Our consumer app: browse restaurants near you, pick a meal, check out, enjoy.</dd>
  <dt>DropBoard</dt>
  <dd>The back office: where restaurant partners manage menus, orders, and payouts.</dd>
</dl>

## What's in Droppy

As the design language powering our products, Droppy provides shared components, design tokens and brand assets. This Storybook is the reference for design system components, and this page specifically summarizes our brand identity and principles. The brand is distributed to developer teams via a `@droppy/theme` package.

On this page, you'll find the personality, color system, typography, voice, motion, and accessibility rules shared by everything Mealdrop Corp ships. Brand specificities of both digital products are also covered in the [Product brands](#product-brands) section at the end.

Droppy is warm and unfussy: we get out of your way and make you smile on the way through.
Nothing shouts, not even primary calls to action. A calm mostly-grey interface, a voice that's casual and a little playful, and personality concentrated in small moments rather than sprinkled everywhere.

## Personality

Our products and copy should reflect the following qualities:

<dl>
  <dt>Calm</dt>
  <dd>Quiet, uncluttered, low-contrast. We don't want to jump in people's faces with color or loud typography. Restraint is the default.</dd>
  <dt>Friendly</dt>
  <dd>Approachable and human, never corporate. Rounded forms, sentence-case copy
    that sounds like a helpful friend.</dd>
  <dt>Playful</dt>
  <dd>Small moments of delight and the occasional wink. We want to make people smile without overdoing playfulness. Remember, restraint is the default.</dd>
  <dt>Content-first</dt>
  <dd>Our partners' content needs to shine, so we give it room to breathe. In MealDrop that
    means the food does the tempting. In DropBoard it means the numbers stay legible.</dd>
</dl>

These qualities are hierarchical, so always prioritise calmness over friendliness, etc.


## Color

Droppy's palette is a large neutral system with a few small, deliberate splashes of color.
The governing idea: grey does the work; color marks moments.

Our palette includes the following roles for you to choose from. Match brand roles first, and then pick the color that best fits your need.

### Color principles

- **Grey does the work; color marks moments.** Reach for the neutral ramp first. Color is the
  exception that means something.
- **Accents are surfaces, not ink.** Soft green and pale blue are backgrounds that carry
  dark text, but they're too light to draw a legible line, border, or icon. Exceptions can be made when necessary (focus rings or review text for better visual coherence).
- **The brand color should not blend into functional UI.** Teal communicates the brand identity, so keeping it off the UI is what lets it feel special.
- **Text color communicates state, not hierarchy.** Text color changes should be reserved for error and disabled states. Hierarchy is achieved through text size and weight instead.

## Typography

The Droppy brand uses one fontface for display, and another for body copy and UI. Both are open-source Google fonts.

<dl>
  <dt>Montserrat</dt>
  <dd>Display face for headings and wordmarks. Weights 400/500/700/900.</dd>
  <dt>Hind</dt>
  <dd>Text and UI face for body copy, labels, and buttons. Weights 400/500.</dd>
</dl>

The type scale is designed to allow rich size hierarchy. Headings are bold by default, and other types have a regular weight, but you may freely override weights to emphasize words within a heading or text body.

| Element | Size (rem) | Default Weight | Tracking | Suggested use |
| ------- | ---------: | -----: | -------: | ------------- | 
| h1      | 2.74       | 700    | -2px     | Heading |
| h2      | 2.19       | 700    | -2px     | Heading |
| h3      | 1.75       | 700    | 0px      | Heading |
| h4      | 1.4        | 700    | 0px      | Product wordmark; overlay and section titles |
| body    | 1.125      | 400    | 0px      | Copy |
| small   | 1          | 400    | 0px      | Buttons, inputs, field labels |
| xs      | 0.9        | 400    | 0px      | UI labels and captions | 
| xxs     | 0.72       | 400    | 0px      | Captions | 

The scale stops at h4. There is no fifth heading step: a title that needs to sit below h4 takes the heading face at a body-scale size rather than inventing one.


## Voice & tone

Droppy talks like a friendly, slightly cheeky human; never like a corporation. Neither friendliness
nor cheekiness should get in the way of users reaching their goals without stress, though. The primary
brand principle remains calmness. Use jokes or casual language in moderation, where it elevates the
experience (e.g. 404 page) without undermining clarity (e.g. a payment selection page).

- **Sentence case everywhere.** Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only. Never Title
  Case, never ALL CAPS.
- **Short and direct.** "View all restaurants." "Complete order." "Your cart is empty." Say
  the thing.
- **Warm, with room for a wink.** Empty and innocuous error states are where personality lives; a
  light joke is welcome when it doesn't get in the way.
- **Punctuation with feeling.** A plain period for the everyday; an exclamation for genuine
  celebration or a real problem ("Order confirmed!", "Something went wrong!").

Here are some examples of voice and tone in action:

| Moment         | Say this                                   | Not this                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Primary CTA    | View all restaurants                       | VIEW ALL RESTAURANTS / Click here     |
| Empty category | This is not the food you're looking for.   | No results found.                     |
| 500 error      | Our bad, something went wrong on our side. | Error 500: Internal Server Error      |
| 404            | This page doesn't exist, keep looking.     | The requested resource was not found. |
| Loading        | Looking for some food…                     | Loading…                              |
| Success        | Order confirmed!                           | Transaction complete.                 |

## Imagery & illustration

The use of imagery differs between customer and partner centric products. Here are a few general principles:

| When to apply? | Principle | 
| --------: | :------------- |
| Customer-facing | Imagery is the primary point of focus: product photography and illustrations should be ever present. |
| Customer-facing | Photography should be bright, real, and appetizing. |
| Customer-facing | Partner restaurant identities should be respected and highlighted with good photography and logotype. |
| Partner-facing | Imagery should be toned down to brand moments, so partners can focus on their data and orders. |
| Both | Brand illustrations should be flat, friendly and simple SVG scenes. |
| Both | Animations should accompany application states to make them more lively and warm. |


## Motion

Motion is brief, functional, and calm.

- **Interactions run short and ease-in** (roughly 150–300ms). Buttons, hovers, transitions.
- **Theme switch cross-fades over 250ms.** A small, deliberate delight; keep it.
- **Character animation belongs to the brand marks.** Logo animations and branded spinners
  are Droppy being playful about itself; don't replicate that energy on ordinary controls.
- **Ambient motion is a marketing indulgence.** A slow hero pan is fine on a marketing
  surface; it doesn't belong inside a product's working screens.
- **Elevation means floating, not importance.** Shadows are for things that hover above the
  page (modals, sidebars, hover lifts). Resting cards stay flat, separated by a background
  shift, not a shadow.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the brand's calm, not an afterthought.

- **One focus ring, everywhere.** A single blue two-layer ring (thin ring + soft halo) is the
  focus treatment for every control, in every product. Don't invent per-component focus styles.
- **Legible by construction.** Because the green and blue accents are surfaces carrying dark
  text (never colored text on white), they stay readable by design. Keep them that way.
- **Never rely on color alone.** Hierarchy and meaning come through size, weight, and copy;
  color is a supporting signal, not the only one. Bring iconography to the rescue when needed.
- **Sentence case aids scanning** and keeps the tone human. It's an accessibility choice as
  much as a style one.
- **Both light and dark themes are designed to meet contrast** on their own terms.

## Product brands

Below are specifics about how each product embodies the brand.

### MealDrop

The consumer food-delivery app — and the product where Droppy's playfulness runs warmest.

- **Mascot.** MealDrop's mark is a friendly creature, a two-eared fox built from soft
  shapes. The mascot is where Droppy's teal identity ramp lives.
- **Wordmark.** "MealDrop" must always show in the display face (Montserrat) with
  normal tracking (the tight heading tracking is only needed for sentence headings, not wordmark).
- **The shine.** The logo plays a staggered "shine", where each shape briefly and slowly brightens.
  Opt-in and celebratory, the shine also fires on hover to encompass playfulness. It belongs to brand
  identity and should not be imitated in ordinary UI.
- **Appetite appeal.** The food is the star: real, bright food photography needs plenty of UI
  headroom to seduce customers. Character moments, e.g. the chomping "Looking for
  some food…" spinner, carry the personality.


### DropBoard

The back office for restaurant partners shares much design language, but further emphasizes calmness.
Partners are working, not browsing for culinary experiences.

- **Wordmark only.** "DropBoard", one word, camelCase, set in the display face with normal
  tracking. There is no DropBoard mascot; teal appears only in the wordmark and the
  loading spinner.
- **Denser, still calm.** Tables, filters, and numbers are the content here. The UI stays
  grey and density aims for a soft spot between readability and efficiency.
- **Playfulness dialed down, not off.** Empty states may keep a light touch ("No orders
  yet — enjoy the quiet."), but anything near money or order states is plain and exact
  ("Payout of €1,240.50 scheduled for Friday.").
